What changed in each version of BlockNSFW

1.8.0 Current September 2026
New
Blocked sites are stopped by the browser itself
Until now a blocked site still received your request. Its address was looked up, a connection was made, cookies and the page you came from were sent — and only then did the extension stop the page and show you the blocked screen. You never saw the site, but the site saw you. The whole curated blocklist — 204,223 addresses — is now handed to the browser's own request engine, which refuses those requests outright. Nothing is contacted, and it happens before any script runs.
Filtering now works inside embedded frames
When a page embedded another page inside it, nothing inside that frame was ever examined — no image filter, no AI scanner, no text check. The frame's address was compared against the blocklist and that was all, so any mirror, shortener or redirector the list did not already know went straight through. Sites exist that do nothing but this. Frames are now filtered the same way pages are. Frames under 120 pixels on either side are skipped entirely, so ad slots and tracking pixels cost you nothing.
Fixed
The blocked page no longer leaves the address in your history
The blocked screen carried the address it stopped you from inside its own address — which put it in your browser history. It turned up in history search, was suggested in the address bar as you typed, and was uploaded to your Google account if you have history sync switched on. The words that triggered the block went with it. Those details now travel in memory only, are never written to disk, and are gone when the browser closes.
The image scanner remembers its results again
On Chrome the AI image scanner's 24-hour memory never actually worked. Every attempt to save a result was silently refused, so every page you opened re-scanned every image from scratch. It is stored as intended now, which makes repeat visits noticeably faster.
Changed
Chrome 101 or newer is now required
The new blocking method needs it — on older versions the rules would be thrown away without warning, which is worse than asking. Chrome 101 was released in April 2022. Firefox is unaffected: version 113 was already the minimum.
1.7.7 Included in 1.8.0 September 2026
Fixed
1.7.7 was prepared but never published on its own, so these fixes reached you as part of 1.8.0. They are listed separately as a record of what changed.
Blogspot blogs and some government sites were being blocked
A single entry on the blocklist could block an entire hosting platform. www.blogspot.com was listed, the extension trims www. from addresses, and what was left blocked every Blogger blog on the internet. Separately, gob.mx did the same to every Mexican government site. The list is now checked against the registry of public domain suffixes on every load, so an entry like this is neutralised rather than waiting for a release. If you whitelisted a site to work around it, you can remove that entry.
A blocked word written as a pattern could freeze the page
Certain patterns take many seconds to check against ordinary text, and one saved word of that shape slowed down every page you opened. They are refused when you save them now, with an explanation of what to change. A pattern of that shape you had already saved has stopped matching — rewrite it and save again.
Some sites could slip past the keyword filter
A site avoided the filter entirely just by having an everyday word like “safe”, “help” or “study” somewhere in its address. Those words are now only honoured as a whole part of the address, and eight of the most generic were removed. A site you visit may be newly blocked as a result — whitelist it, and please report it.
A website could put its own content on the blocked page
If you use a custom blocked page written in HTML, the address you were blocked from was dropped into your template without being made safe first — so a site could have written its own text and forms onto the blocked screen, at the extension's own genuine address.
Nothing about your browsing is written into the page any more
Every page you opened had its title and address written into that page's own developer console, four times per visit, where the site's own analytics tools can read it. On AOL and Yahoo, so was what you searched for.
Blocked videos and embedded content are counted again
Three kinds of block never reached your statistics or the toolbar counter, which is why the counter in the corner of the page and the one on the toolbar sometimes disagreed.
The update notice works again
The file the extension checks for new versions had been stuck since June, so the “a new version is available” notice had never appeared for anyone. Edge users who followed it were also being sent to the Chrome Web Store rather than to the Edge add-ons site.
1.7.6 August 2026
New
Choose which DNS resolver checks your browsing
DNS Protection was Cloudflare or nothing. Settings → Protection → DNS Protection now offers four free resolvers, no account needed: Cloudflare for Families (the default, and the fastest), AdGuard DNS Family (also ads and trackers), Mullvad DNS Family (audited no-logging) and the CleanBrowsing Adult Filter (adult content only). Queries never pass through a server of ours.
Or use any resolver you like
Picking Custom reveals an address box that takes any DNS-over-HTTPS endpoint — your own NextDNS config, a Control D profile, or a resolver you run yourself. It is asked on its own with no fallback, so your choice stands. Test DNS Connection checks that it answers and that it genuinely filters.
Setup now covers blocking beyond this browser
First-run setup has a new, skippable step for network-level blocking: the in-extension DNS check as a toggle, plus copyable resolver addresses to set on your computer or router. That second half covers every app rather than one browser, and it survives BlockNSFW being removed.
Block a whole top-level domain
A line reading .xyz now blocks every site ending in it — the broadest entry the blocklist accepts, and the settings page says so plainly next to it. Images from a blocked TLD are hidden in the page. Requested by Maksim.
Notes are dimmed in your lists
A line starting with # or ! now reads in grey while your entries stay bright, so a list with section headings can be scanned at a glance. Suggested by Maksim.
SafeSearch is locked on DuckDuckGo and Brave
Searches now go to each provider's dedicated filtered address before the page loads, rather than asking the ordinary one to filter afterwards. Filtering there belongs to the search host, so the results-page toggle and links with SafeSearch turned off no longer undo it. DuckDuckGo's plain HTML and Lite pages keep their no-JavaScript layouts. Contributed by oathkeeper-dev.
Image and video search is enforced like web search
On Bing, Ecosia and Presearch, strict mode covered the main web search and nothing else — so the Images, Videos and News tabs stepped straight outside it. Those routes are now enforced too. Contributed by oathkeeper-dev.
Yandex now searches in Family mode
Family mode is set before the first search, image or video request instead of after the results have arrived, and it holds across Yandex's regional domains rather than just the one you started on. The weaker choices on Yandex's own settings page are locked. Your login and session are left untouched. Contributed by oathkeeper-dev.
Firefox 113 or newer is now required
Up from 109. Locking SafeSearch depends on rewriting a request before it leaves the browser, and 113 is the first Firefox that permits it. On anything older the rest of the extension still works, but that enforcement would silently do nothing — so the requirement now says so.
Fixed
Changing detection strictness asked for the whole access code
With the access code on and set to ask on every change, moving image detection strictness down one step made you retype the full random string by hand — the same price as turning blocking off altogether. Nobody doing this was trying to switch the AI off; they were trying to stop an ordinary photo being blurred. The code is there to guard the way out, and a sensitivity dial is not one: at Relaxed the blocker is still running and still catches clearly explicit images. Image strictness, AI text strictness and image filtering no longer ask for the code on either setting. Your PIN still applies to all three, and every other lock is unchanged.
The access code did not guard "unblock this site"
If you had set an access code — the long random string you retype by hand, on top of your PIN — it was only ever asked for on the settings page. Unblocking a site from the toolbar popup asked for the four-digit PIN and nothing else, and unblocking a site opens every page on it, so the strongest lock the extension offers was missing from the one place most likely to be used in a weak moment. Whitelisting a site by hand and turning SafeSearch off went the same way. The popup now asks for the code exactly as the settings page does, and unblocking a whole site is treated as one of the decisive actions — it asks for the code even on the default setting. Unblocking a single page is narrower and is not. Re-blocking a site still needs only the PIN: tightening protection is never made harder. Reported by Pattrattbarr (#29).
DNS Protection could stop working without saying so
A failed lookup — unreachable resolver, rate limit, timeout — was treated like a clean one, so the domain was remembered as safe while Settings still reported DNS as active. Failures are now told apart and never remembered, a second resolver is tried, and one that keeps failing is rested. Test DNS Connection also checks that filtering really works.
Local sites, including your own dev server, were blocked
Local addresses — localhost, home-network devices, intranet addresses — were blocked on sight by two separate layers, and could not be whitelisted around it either. The DNS check asked a public resolver about an address only your own machine knows; the page scan blocked on one adult keyword in a title, which any site about blocking adult content trips. Both skip local addresses now, your own blocked-site rules still apply there, and localhost is whitelistable.
Sorting filed pattern entries under their punctuation
A /regex/ entry was sorted under its opening slash, so patterns clumped at the top of a saved list and dragged the notes written beneath them along. Entries are now filed under their first letter — /apricots?/ sorts beside apricots. Reported by Maksim.
A blocklist entry written with a leading dot could break image blocking
An entry such as .xyz was handed to the browser as a domain to block images from, which it is not. Image blocking uses one rule shared by every blocked site, so a single entry like that could stop it working for all the rest.
DuckDuckGo's Images and Videos tabs were not filtered at all
A blocked word stopped the matching web results, then the same search on the Images or Videos tab showed everything — the filter was looking for parts of an old layout that no longer exist. Both tabs are filtered now, along with the thumbnail rows on the All tab, and a blocked image takes its whole tile with it.
A search result that arrived late was never filtered
DuckDuckGo builds its results row by row, and a row was being marked as inspected before its title had loaded — so whichever row was slowest survived. Rows are now re-examined until they are actually readable.
Your own blocked words were ignored in image search
In image results, a word from your own list only counted when the image filter was set to Strict; Moderate and Lenient consulted the built-in list instead. A word you typed yourself is an instruction rather than a guess, so it now applies at every level.
"Go Back" on the blocked page did nothing
The button's click was being discarded outright by the extension's own security policy. It works now — and when there is nothing in the tab's history to go back to, it leaves the page instead of sitting there.
Explicit images on X were spotted but left unblurred
The AI filter was recognising them — the blocks showed up in your stats — and then the blur came straight back off. X reuses picture frames as you scroll, so a verdict for one image could land on the next and uncover it. Verdicts now stick to the exact image they were about. Reported by tachangan.
The audit log's page buttons did nothing
Next, previous, first, last and the numbered pages were all being discarded by the extension's own security policy, the same way the blocked page's Go Back was — the log was stuck showing its first 20 entries. They work now. Reported by tachangan.
1.7.5 August 2026
New
Blocked search results are removed, not papered over
Every blocked result used to be replaced with a large card carrying the extension's logo and a BLOCKED badge. That was bearable for two results in ten and became a wall once image and video results started being filtered too. Blocked results are now simply removed, and one line above the results accounts for them: "3 results blocked by BlockNSFW" — one line however many were blocked. Image and video results just go; a gap in a grid reads as fewer results, while a grid of placeholders reads as an error. Reported by a user moving over from uBlacklist.
You choose what a blocked result leaves behind
Settings → Customization → Blocked Search Results. Keep the quiet removal, or pick Show a notice in place to bring the old per-result card back for web results — a removed result is indistinguishable from one that never existed, and that per-position evidence is the point in an accountability setup. A second switch turns the summary line off entirely. Image and video results are always removed either way.
A count of what was blocked
Nothing outside the page ever showed the extension was working. The toolbar icon now carries the number of things blocked in that tab — search results, filtered images — capped at 99+, and cleared when you navigate. If you would rather have it in the page, Where To Show The Count offers a small floating pill in the corner instead. Clicking the pill lists the sites the blocks came from, busiest first. Those listed sites are plain text: they are not links and lead nowhere.
Follow blocklists other people maintain
Settings → Customization → Subscribed Lists. Give it the address of a ruleset file and BlockNSFW downloads it, blocks everything on it alongside your own entries, and checks for changes once a day. When the author adds new sites, you get them without doing anything. uBlacklist ruleset files work as they are, so addresses you already follow there can be pasted straight in, and one-click subscribe buttons on list authors' sites open Settings with the address filled in ready for you to confirm. A subscribed list can only add blocks. It can never whitelist a site, unblock anything, or change a setting. Turning one off asks for your PIN. Requested by a user.
Comments in your lists
A line starting with # or ! is now a note rather than an entry, in all three boxes — blocklist, blocked words and trusted sites. Both markers work because the tools people arrive from disagree: uBlacklist uses #, uBlock Origin and AdGuard use !. Notes stay attached to the entry written below them when the list is sorted, so they work as section headings, and they survive Import and Export. To block a word that genuinely starts with one of those characters, put a backslash first — \#nsfw. Any such entry already in your list was converted for you, so nothing you saved changes meaning. Requested by a user.
A nudge to pin the extension
Chrome tucks unpinned extensions behind the puzzle-piece menu, which hides the icon, the count on it, and the quickest route to unblocking a site. Settings now shows a dismissible reminder of how to pin it — and only when it is genuinely unpinned.
Fixed
AI image blocking never worked on Firefox
Turning the beta on did nothing on Firefox: no image was ever checked and none was ever blurred. Firefox and Chrome run an extension's background code in two different kinds of container, and the step that loads the image model only ever worked in Chrome's — so on Firefox the model was never there to ask. Nothing looked wrong, which is why this went unnoticed for so long: when the model is unavailable the images are shown rather than left as blank holes, so a page with the filter on looked exactly like a page with nothing to block. The model now loads on Firefox too. Chrome and Edge were never affected.
Firefox was matching against a shorter blocklist than Chrome
The same underlying cause, with a quieter symptom. Three pieces of shared matching logic never reached Firefox's background, and each had a smaller fallback it used instead — so Firefox checked addresses against a 27-word list rather than the full one, missed look-alike domains written in other alphabets, and quietly ignored the path on a whitelist entry, meaning allow this one section of a site allowed nothing at all. All three are in place now.
"Unblock this website" whitelisted the extension instead of the site
Used from a blocked page, the popup read the address of the tab it was open on — which is the extension's own blocked page, not the site that was blocked. The entry it saved matched nothing, so the whitelist appeared to accept it while the site stayed blocked, with nothing to explain why. Reported in #26 on Firefox, though the same path affected Chrome and Edge.
1.7.4 17 August 2026
New
Take BlockNSFW beyond this browser
Android blocks adult sites across your whole phone, not just one browser — get it on Google Play. Desktop Guard (beta) is a native companion app for Windows and Linux that force-installs the extension so the browser's Remove and Disable buttons are greyed out, and re-applies protection if anything tampers with it. Uninstalling needs a password held by a parent or accountability partner — learn more.
Access Code
An optional second lock on top of your PIN. A random code appears and you have to retype it by hand — copy and paste are switched off, and a typo gives you a fresh code. Choose 32, 64, 128 or 256 characters. By default it only guards the big moments (turning blocking off, clearing your PIN), so day-to-day edits stay quick; there's a switch to require it for every change. It isn't about secrecy — it's about giving yourself a minute to think. Under Security.
Sites you block are now blocked as a source of images
Adding a site to your blocklist stopped you visiting it, but its pictures still turned up everywhere else, most obviously sitting in image search results. Blocking a site now hides its results in image search and hides its images wherever they're embedded or hotlinked, and the picture itself is refused before it downloads. Requested by a user.
Blocked sites can be patterns too
Alongside wildcards, the blocklist now takes a regular expression for the address, /example\.(net|org)/, or for the page title, title/Example Domain/. Same syntax as uBlacklist, so lists you already keep will work. Address patterns block before the page loads; title patterns catch it just after.
Blocked words can be patterns
Instead of listing every spelling of a word, write one line that covers them all. Wrap it in slashes to make it a pattern: /p[o0]rn/ catches "porn" and "p0rn", and /escort(s|ing)?/ catches "escort", "escorts" and "escorting". Plain words still work exactly as before. Patterns are checked when you save, so a mistake tells you what's wrong instead of quietly failing, and one slow enough to freeze pages is refused.
Import and export your lists
Your blocklist, blocked words and trusted sites now have Import and Export buttons. Export saves a file you can keep as a backup or open in a spreadsheet; import accepts .csv or .txt and adds to what you already have rather than replacing it. Imported entries appear in the box first, so you can look them over before hitting Save.
Pages no longer freeze while loading
On Firefox especially, opening a page could stall for about a second while the extension got itself ready, which felt like the browser had locked up. That pause is gone: on the same test page the longest frozen moment dropped from roughly 1.2 seconds to under a twentieth of a second. The same fix applies to Chrome, and nothing about what gets blocked has changed. Contributed by oathkeeper-dev.
Fixed
Names like "Jerome" are no longer blocked
An adult site name buried inside an ordinary word was being treated as a match, so a news page about Jerome Powell was blocked outright and searching for the name blocked the results. Site names now have to stand on their own to count.
A search engine's own safety filter no longer looks like adult content
On 4get.ca, switching its filter on puts the word "nsfw" in the address, and the page was blocked for saying it while doing exactly what you'd want. Settings in a web address are now read as switches rather than as content.
Your settings are harder to unlock in a weak moment
Some settings could previously be changed without your PIN, including deleting your own blocked words, turning off DNS Protection, and lowering AI strictness. Everything that weakens protection now asks for your PIN first. Adding words, tightening settings and turning protections on stay instant, as they should.
Setting up is quicker and uses less data
A fresh install downloaded the whole blocklist straight away, even though the very same list already ships inside the extension. It now starts from the copy it already has and only fetches an update when one is genuinely due, so setup finishes sooner and costs you several megabytes less.
Tidier lists
Your blocklist, blocked words and trusted sites are now sorted A–Z and de-duplicated when you save, so pasting in a long list no longer leaves a mess.
AI Text Protection now asks for a second opinion
The text AI was flagging ordinary sites such as YouTube, so it no longer blocks a page on its own. A page is blocked only when both its text and an image on it look adult, which means the AI Image Blocker needs to be on for this to do anything. It catches less for now; the model is being retrained.
