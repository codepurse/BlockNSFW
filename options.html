The biggest update since BlockNSFW began. Read about 2.0

  • The Pact New

    Choose a wait, from 15 minutes to 3 days. While a pact is on, every change that loosens protection waits it out before it happens, and you can cancel it any time before then. Making protection stronger never waits. It’s in Security › The Pact.

  • Gateways New

    Name the sites where it tends to start, such as Instagram Explore or Reddit. Opening one asks you to pause for ten seconds first, with your own words, and during your hard hours they stay closed. Protection › Gateways.

  • Storm Mode and risk hours New

  • A new look, in light and dark New

  • A friend can hold the key New

    Someone you trust scans a code into the authenticator app on their phone. When you need a change without the wait, you ask them for the six-digit code it shows. They never see your browsing, and nothing goes through a server.

  • Your own words on the blocked page New

    Write your plan, a note to yourself and someone to reach, in a calm moment. A blocked page shows them first, and offers to wait out the next ten or twenty minutes with you.

  • After a slip New

    The popup’s “I slipped” link opens a short, plain page. It counts as one day, never a reset, and offers what would help next time.

  • Your week New

    Every Sunday evening, a short page about your week: each time you stopped at the door or waited it out, drawn day by day, three numbers beside the week before, and one thing to try next. The popup says when it’s ready.

  • Days kept

    Statistics counts the days in the last 30 with protection on all day and no slip, beside your runs and the moments you waited out.

  • Supporter, for going further New

  • Your PIN is stored safely

    It is kept as a salted hash instead of plain text, and five wrong tries lock PIN entry for a while. A PIN from an older version keeps working.

  • The popup says whether you’re protected

    It opens on a deep green band that says “Protected” and holds the protection switch. When protection is off the band turns plain and says so.

  • The blocked page no longer shows the blocked address

    It shows the site’s ending only now, and never the words a page matched. The audit log shows a site’s name instead of its full address.

  • “Held at the second door.”

    The Classic blocked page says why first: it draws the six layers of protection as six doors and shows which one held the page, with one plain sentence on what that means. The other designs keep everything they do, in the new colours.

What happens when an image is flagged

It’s blurred in place, so it can’t be seen while the rest of the page keeps working. Nothing is removed or uploaded; the blur happens on your device. If the AI later decides the image is safe, the blur comes off by itself.

AI text blocker beta

A small on-device model reads each page’s text, to catch adult sites that keyword and blocklist filters miss. It reads English and most European languages best; Japanese, Chinese and Russian pages are left to the other filters for now. Nothing is uploaded.

DNS protection

Asks a family-safe DNS resolver about each site before the page loads, as a second layer on top of the blocklist.

Checks sites the local blocklist hasn’t already decided on with the resolver you choose below.

Turn on DNS filtering to choose a resolver.

Any DoH address: your own NextDNS setup, a Control D profile, or a resolver you run yourself. Press Test DNS connection afterwards; it checks that the address answers and that it really filters adult domains.

Where DNS lookups go

Queries go straight from your browser to the resolver you pick; they never pass through BlockNSFW’s servers. Only sites the local blocklist hasn’t already decided on are looked up, and each answer is kept for an hour. If a built-in resolver doesn’t answer, BlockNSFW asks a second one before giving up, so this layer doesn’t fail silently. A custom resolver is asked on its own, and nothing else.

Google, Bing, DuckDuckGo, Yahoo, Brave, Ecosia, Qwant, AOL Search, Presearch and Yandex. DuckDuckGo and Brave go through their dedicated safe sites; on the others strict filtering is enforced as each request is made, and their own SafeSearch controls are locked where that’s possible.

Words or phrases that always block a page they appear on. One per line. A line wrapped in slashes is a regular expression, so one line can cover many spellings: /p[o0]rn/ catches “porn” and “p0rn”, and /escort(s|ing)?/ catches all three forms. Capitals don’t matter either way. Patterns are checked when you save, and one that is invalid or slow enough to freeze pages is refused, with the reason.

          

          

          
Notes and headings in the list
A line that starts with # or ! is a note, ignored when matching. Notes never move when you save; only the words between two notes are sorted, so a note works as a heading. To block a word that really starts with one of those, put a backslash first: \#nsfw.

          

            
            

          

          

            
            
            
            
          

          

            
            
            
          

          

        


        

          

          

          
One entry per line. Four kinds of entry work:

          

          

          

            
Sites, with wildcards
pornhub.com · *.inappropriate.net · example.com/adult/*

            
A whole top-level domain
.xyz or xyz blocks every site that ends in it. Some endings are used almost only for spam and malware, and this covers all of them at once, which also makes it the broadest entry you can write, so check it’s what you want. Images from a blocked ending are hidden on the page rather than stopped on the network.

            
A regular expression on the address
/example\.(net|org)/

            
A regular expression on the page title
title/Example Domain/. Title patterns match once the page loads, so the page shows briefly before it’s held; address patterns hold it before it loads at all.

          

          
How your entries are checked
Patterns are checked when you save, and one that is invalid or slow enough to freeze pages is refused, with the reason. A site you add here is also blocked as a source of images: its results are hidden in image search, and its pictures are blocked wherever they appear.
